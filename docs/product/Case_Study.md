# Package Forge — requirements before price

## Problem and product judgment

A buyer can select a cheaper plan while accidentally removing an approval step, or read an annual equivalent as a monthly bill. Package Forge uses an explicit needs worksheet and a separate renewal review so the choice can be explained before a local simulation is confirmed.

Mo owns product/program direction; AI assisted implementation and verification. The scope is an original fictional prototype. No human interviews, owner endorsement, manual-coding claim, revenue effect, willingness-to-pay evidence or customer adoption is claimed.

## The alternative and tradeoff

A price-led table minimizes steps but leaves capability and capacity failures implicit. An opaque wizard could recommend a package without exposing the requirement boundary. The chosen worksheet makes all three packages visible, annotates missing requirements, and recommends only the lowest-cost fit. A separate scope review adds effort when requirements change, which is justified because removing approvals changes the decision itself.

Cedar's 8 editors and 24 free viewers require approvals: Studio is the lowest-cost fit at $96/month. Annual Studio is $960 paid annually and $80 equivalent/month, not twelve $80 bills. Harbor requires audit export: Archive costs $2,880 annually. Widefield exceeds all capacities: there is honestly no fit. Kite's intentionally smaller renewal team can choose Base at $0, after seeing lost approvals and capacities 20→3 editors and 200→20 viewers.

## Commitment and recovery

The active Studio baseline stays separate from a scheduled October31 change. Versioned quotes preserve exact assumptions and arithmetic; changing the draft cannot rewrite earlier commitments. Cancel-preview retains the schedule. Cancel-scheduled removes only that schedule and never restores an older one. A corrupt local record is preserved until explicit reset. These boundaries prioritize an understandable consequence over generic Undo everywhere.

## Evidence and what remains unknown

Software checks establish calculation, fit, state integrity and reproducible browser behavior, detailed in [Validation](Validation.md). Human evaluation is proposed: ask a participant to select Cedar's tier, explain annual cash commitment and describe Kite's losses and effective date. Success would be a correct explanation without prompting; no participant outcomes have been observed.
